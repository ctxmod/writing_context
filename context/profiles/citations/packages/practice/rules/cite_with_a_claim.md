# Cite with a Claim

A citation earns its place by supporting a statement the reader can
check. A bare pointer that asserts nothing — "following established
precedent `[@x]`" — hands the reader a name but no claim to evaluate.

## Rationale

State, in at least half a sentence, what the cited work did or found,
then cite it: "Shared context must stay artifact-agnostic
`[@writing_context_sources_ctxmod_source_example_example]`" rather than "per
established doctrine
`[@writing_context_sources_ctxmod_source_example_example]`". If the cited work's
contribution does not fit the current argument, the citation belongs
elsewhere or not at all.

Avoid sequential listing ("X says A. Y says B. Z says C."); synthesize
relationships, and vary narrative (`@key` in prose) versus parenthetical
(`[@key]`) placement instead of defaulting to sentence-final brackets.
