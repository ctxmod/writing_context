# Clarity

Writing an agent consumes prioritizes clarity over impressiveness. The
agent takes the same process every run; the document is a specification
of that process, not a persuasion piece.

## Direct communication

State claims directly and give the reader random access: any section
should be understandable without reading everything before it. Delete
"it is important to note", "essentially", and every sentence whose job
is announcing the next one.

## Active voice

Prefer active voice for directness; reserve passive for emphasizing the
object or an unknown agent. Target roughly 80% active. "The agent reads
the file", not "the file is read by the agent".

## Sentence construction

Mix sentence lengths: roughly 70% medium (15–25 words), 20% short
(8–14), 10% long (26–35). Avoid uniform length (mechanical rhythm),
choppiness, and long unparsable chains. Mix simple, compound, and
complex sentence types, and vary openings (transitional, adverbial,
prepositional, subject-first). Never repeat the same transition word
consecutively.

## Hedging

Hedge only genuinely uncertain claims: approximators ("approximately",
"roughly") and shields ("may", "suggests", "tends to"), about one per
fifty words. Hedging a claim the evidence settles adds noise without
honesty.

## Paragraphs and headings

Paragraphs run three to eight sentences with a topic sentence and one
main idea each. Headings are specific, informative, concise, and
parallel — main headings four to eight words, subheadings three to six;
vague headings ("General", "Various", "Miscellaneous") are renamed. At
most three heading levels; deeper nesting is a restructuring signal.

## Word choice

Use a technical term when it adds precision and define it on first use.
Reject terms that signal group membership rather than precision, and
formulaic vocabulary ("delve", "underscore", "tapestry", "realm",
"crucial", "leverage") — the anti-patterns list carries the full
replacements.
